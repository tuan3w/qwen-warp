# Fastest single-3090 decode of Swift-1.5-Qwen3.8-27b-INT4 (W4A16 g128 + MTP): does a fus...

## Raw

Fastest single-3090 decode of Swift-1.5-Qwen3.8-27b-INT4 (W4A16 g128 + MTP): does a fused/persistent megakernel beat vLLM's CUDA-graph decode, and how much of the gain is persistence vs fusion/GEMV quality?

## Sharpened

Target: one RTX 3090 (sm_86, 936 GB/s, 82 SMs, 6 MB L2, <=100 KB smem/SM), model Swift-1.5-Qwen3.8-27b-INT4 (compressed-tensors W4A16 g128 symmetric; embed/lm_head/MTP/in_proj_a,b are BF16), vLLM-integrated. Goal: highest tokens/s of single-stream (bs=1) decode, with and without MTP speculation, at bounded quality loss.

**Roofline (our arithmetic from safetensors headers, `/tmp/swift_tensors.json`; plain decode reads each weight once per forward):** layer weights+scales 12.59 GB + BF16 lm_head 2.54 GB = 15.13 GB -> 61.9 tok/s at 100% of 936 GB/s, ~55 at 90%. DeltaNet fp32 state 48 x 3.15 MB read+write = 0.30 GB/token (2%). KV (16 layers x 2 x 4 x 256 x 2 B) = 32 KB/token FP8 or 64 KB BF16: at 8K ctx 0.27-0.54 GB. MTP head (BF16, not quantised) adds 0.85 GB + a full lm_head read per draft step unless the draft vocab is restricted (open-jet uses a 32k draft vocab).

**Falsifiable sub-questions**
1. Where does stock vLLM (Marlin + CUDA graphs, MTP k=1..3) sit against that roofline on this 3090, and which of {GEMV efficiency, inter-kernel gaps, non-GEMV ops, lm_head, sampling/host} is the gap? (Measure, do not assume.)
2. Persistence vs fusion: at our shapes, does a persistent kernel with cross-barrier weight prefetch beat the same kernels in a CUDA graph with PDL by more than 5%? Prior: msaroufim/megakernels-vs-cuda-graphs measured 0.7-4.6% (Llama-1B, H100/H200); bonsai-turbo (Qwen3.6-27B-shape hybrid) measured graph 151 vs megakernel 150 tok/s on H100. We bet the same holds here.
3. How much of 936 GB/s can a W4 g128 GEMV sustain at [5120x17408]-class shapes on sm_86 (clean-room kernel vs Marlin vs copy peak)?
4. MTP verify: cost of T=1+k rows vs 1 row for the whole step (weights read once), DeltaNet rollback cost (snapshots vs lazy replay), draft cost with restricted vocab, and real acceptance on Swift's MTP head (does Swift's post-training keep the head aligned?).
5. lm_head: BF16 2.54 GB is 17% of bytes. INT8/INT4 lm_head quality loss (KL / top-1) vs speed.

Prior art: [related-work/open-jet.md](../related-work/open-jet.md) (CUDA, sm_86, GGUF Q4_K_M, 78 tok/s reasoning with MTP, AGPL-3.0: design reference only, write clean-room), [related-work/tpu-megakernels.md](../related-work/tpu-megakernels.md) (TPU; tile-stream ring with cross-op lookahead, sequential DeltaNet verify with per-token snapshots), vendor/bonsai-turbo (Apache-2.0, Qwen3.6-27B-shape batch-1 engine, graph vs megakernel modes), vendor/megakernels-vs-cuda-graphs.

Interpretations assumed: "for vllm" = a plugin/custom op that serves this model through `vllm serve` on the 3090 (seam: `PluggableLayer.register_oot` for GDN layers or `ModelRegistry.register_model` for the whole decoder stack; see MapVllm2 report in the experiment folder), with a standalone engine first because it iterates faster. "Fastest possible" = tok/s at bs=1 under a fixed quality bound, not throughput at high concurrency.

## Plan

Cheapest kill test (E1, no weights needed): synthetic W4 stream sweep on the 3090: copy peak, GEMV-per-kernel in a CUDA graph, same with PDL, persistent with barriers, persistent with cross-barrier prefetch, over the real per-token matrix sequence. If persistence does not beat graph+PDL by >5%, the program becomes "fused kernel set + CUDA graph", still integrated into vLLM.
Baseline (E0): vLLM stock Swift INT4 bs=1, MTP k=0..3, same prompts, 3 interleaved repeats.
Then: correctness oracle (torch reference of the text decoder with fp32 state), per-op kernels, whole-step engine, MTP verify, vLLM plugin, then an autonomous-campaign over the engine (metric tok/s, held-out = different context/depth, check = KL vs oracle).
